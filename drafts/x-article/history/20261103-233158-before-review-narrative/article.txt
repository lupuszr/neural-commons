An AI changed the weights. Show the evidence.

What changed, what passed, what failed—and what the next agent can inherit.

[COVER: assets/11-keynote-cover.png]

Imagine reviewing a model change. The agent reports better accuracy. There is a chart. The bar goes up.

Which weights changed? Which examples were used to choose the edit? What got worse? Can someone else reproduce the result?

Those questions determine whether another engineer—or another agent—can build on the work.

Neural Commons is an experiment in making that handoff inspectable: direct weight edits, recorded evaluations, and a shared history through OriginTrail DKG. We now have a two-controller experiment, an application to inspect it, and a backend for continuing the search.

Start with the failure.

One early edit made the model more confident without improving accuracy. Another improved its target task while damaging a sentiment probe.

Both results remain in the record. The second failure led to a required regression guard.

A higher target score tells you something useful. You still have to check what the change broke.

Make the change small enough to inspect.

In the first successful AG News experiment, a Codex controller requested as GPT-6 Astra directly edited 2,268 BF16 weights in SmolLM2-135M-Instruct.

The host supplied labeled development examples and activation contrasts. The agent inspected weights, chose edits and coefficients, and tested 13 proposals. A separate selection split chose the candidate before the final test. No gradients or optimizer were used.

On a fixed 1,000-example AG News test subset, accuracy rose from 38.7% to 80.0%: 413 more correct answers.

Caption: First round: 2,268 edited weights, +41.3 percentage points on a fixed four-way classification task.

The known 56-example sentiment guard stayed unchanged. Another local DKG participant reproduced the scores and the exact evaluator-output hash.

Now there is something to review: a numerical diff, a measured effect, a regression check and a reproduced result.

The experiment was inspired by Rikiya Takehi’s post (https://x.com/rikiyatakehi/status/2117313742891327994) and Sparks of Weight-Space Programming (https://rikiyat.github.io/blog/weight-space-programming/), by Takehi, Jayden Teoh, Alex Zhang and Omar Khattab. Neural Commons explores collaboration around these edits; the results here are our own task-specific experiments.

Hand it to the next agent.

Suppose the next agent starts tomorrow. It needs the exact checkpoint, the previous hypothesis, the evaluation inputs and the numerical diff. A summary saying “accuracy improved” leaves too much to guess.

GPT-6.1 Sol received that material through DKG. Its base checkpoint ID exactly matched Astra’s candidate. Every new proposal started from the inherited model.

Sol tested 15 proposals on fresh development examples. A separate selection split chose an edit to another 1,152 BF16 values. Some overlap the first patch, so the counts cannot be added as unique weights.

[INSERT IMAGE: assets/13-keynote-continuation.png]
Caption: Second round: original 39.8%, inherited 84.2%, after Sol 84.5%. All three used the same fresh 1,000-example final set, different from the first chart.

The second edit corrected 22 errors and introduced 19. The net gain was 0.3 percentage points. The sentiment guard stayed unchanged. Another local participant reproduced the frozen second-stage scores and output hash.

Keep the rule that the result failed.

The original acceptance gate required a one-point improvement. The second edit failed it.

After seeing the measurements, we lowered the threshold to 0.2 points and reassessed the same result. That changed the acceptance decision. It did not change the experiment.

The record preserves both decisions. The paired 95% interval is approximately −0.96 to +1.56 percentage points, so the accuracy gain is not statistically conclusive.

The checkpoint handoff and local reproduction worked. A reliable incremental accuracy improvement still needs stronger evidence.

Give the evidence an address.

For another participant to repeat the evaluation, the model, patch, inputs and policy must remain identifiable and retrievable.

@origin_trail (https://x.com/origin_trail)’s Decentralized Knowledge Graph connects those artifacts with their proposals, evaluations and reviews:

• Record the edit. Preserve the changed values and their parent checkpoint.
• Freeze the experiment. Bind the checkpoint, inputs and evaluation policy to content hashes.
• Repeat the evaluation. Retrieve the artifacts, verify them and compare the output.
• Continue from that checkpoint. Give the next controller the exact model and its recorded history.

Neural Commons handles tensor edits and evaluation. DKG provides artifact transport, authorization and shared provenance. Weights remain in checkpoint files; the graph connects the evidence.

These LLM handoffs used separate participant identities and stores on one physical machine. Independent institutional validation and public-chain finality remain unproven.

Make the workflow usable.

[INSERT IMAGE: assets/14-workspace.png]
Caption: The current application, displaying the recorded first-round AG News result.

The workspace lets you compare checkpoints, inspect changes, open evidence and start a continuation from an eligible candidate.

Continue from candidate starts a background job with a chosen controller, fresh task inputs and a budget. Job state survives browser closure and server restart. You can cancel, resume an unfinished search, or retry importing a frozen candidate.

Final evaluation and DKG sharing remain explicit actions. Finishing a search produces a candidate ready for testing.

Local automated checks covered the UI and job lifecycle, including restart and cancellation, using an inert worker. They did not make a new live model call. The earlier recorded experiments supply the weight-editing evidence.

Leave the next contributor a testable claim.

These results cover a small model, a fixed prompt and forced-choice classification on length-filtered benchmark subsets. They do not establish better general conversation, preservation of every capability, or an advantage over fine-tuning. Pretraining exposure cannot be excluded.

Broader regression tests and independent participants are the next work.

Every proposed improvement should arrive with enough evidence for someone else to challenge it, repeat it and decide whether to continue from it.

Inspect the diff. Run the evaluation. Build the next step.
